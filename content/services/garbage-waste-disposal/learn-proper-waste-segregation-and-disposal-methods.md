# Learn Proper Waste Segregation and Disposal Methods

Basic waste segregation rules that apply in Marinduque under the national solid waste law (RA 9003), and who enforces them locally.

Under the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act (RA 9003), households and establishments must segregate waste at source. Your barangay and municipality enforce these rules, usually through the MENRO.

## Who can use this

Every household, business, school, and office in Marinduque.

## The basic rule: segregate at source

- **Biodegradable (nabubulok)** — food scraps, garden waste. Compost at home where possible.
- **Recyclable** — paper, plastic bottles, metals, glass. Sell to junk shops or bring to your barangay's materials recovery facility (MRF), if it has one.
- **Residual** — non-recyclable wrappers, soiled packaging. This is what goes to the disposal facility.
- **Special/hazardous** — batteries, bulbs, electronics, chemicals, medical waste. Do not mix with regular trash — see [special waste collection](/services/garbage-waste-disposal/request-special-waste-collection-hazardous-materials-electronics).

## Steps

1. Set up separate bins or sacks for each type at home.
2. Ask your barangay hall about the local MRF, collection schedule, and any local ordinances (some barangays run "no segregation, no collection" policies).
3. Businesses should coordinate with the MENRO for compliance requirements — segregation compliance is often checked during business permit inspections.

## Fees

Segregation is free. Fines for violations (like illegal dumping or burning) are set by municipal ordinances — ask your barangay or MENRO.
