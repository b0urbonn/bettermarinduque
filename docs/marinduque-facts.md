## Province overview

- Official name: Province of Marinduque. Tagline: "The Heart of the Philippines"
  (heart-shaped island; geographic center of the archipelago). [Wikipedia; marinduque.gov.ph]
- Region: MIMAROPA (formerly Region IV-B), Southwestern Tagalog Region.
- Capital: Boac. Provincial Capitol: Capitol Compound, Brgy. Santol, Boac 4900.
  Office hours Mon–Fri 8:00 AM–5:00 PM. [govserv listing of the Provincial Govt page]
- Founded as a province: February 21, 1921 (separated from Tayabas/Quezon in 1920);
  founding anniversary celebrated every February 21. [marinduque.gov.ph socio-economic profile; PIA]
- Land area: 952.58 km². 6 municipalities, 218 barangays. [Wikipedia / PSA]
- Population: 226,522 as of July 1, 2024. [PSA 2024 Census of Population](https://rssomimaropa.psa.gov.ph/content/official-population-count-province-marinduque-2024-census-population)
- Municipal populations (2024): Boac 54,365; Santa Cruz 51,594; Gasan 35,315;
  Mogpog 32,577; Torrijos 28,535; Buenavista 24,136.
- Barangays per municipality: Boac 61, Santa Cruz 55, Mogpog 37, Gasan 25,
  Torrijos 25, Buenavista 15.
- Legislative board districts: 1st District = Boac, Mogpog, Gasan;
  2nd District = Santa Cruz, Torrijos, Buenavista. One at-large (lone) congressional district.
- Climate: Type III (rainfall fairly evenly distributed, no pronounced dry/wet season).
- Highest point: Mt. Malindig, 1,157 m, potentially active stratovolcano, southern tip (Buenavista).
- Nearby islands: Polo, Maniwaya, Mongpong (northeast, Santa Cruz); Tres Reyes Islands
  and Elephant Island (southwest).
- Language: Tagalog (Marinduque Tagalog dialect, noted for archaic features).
- Economy: agriculture (coconut, rice/palay, banana, corn, root crops), fishing,
  livestock, handicrafts, tourism (peaks during Holy Week), butterfly farming.
  1996 Marcopper mining disaster halted mining on the island.
- Province profile: 3 government-owned hospitals; 3 major seaports; 1 domestic airport.
  [marinduque.gov.ph socio-economic profile]

## Culture

- Moryonan (Moriones Lenten Rites) is a week-long Holy Week religious practice.
  Participants wear carved masks and Roman soldier garb during processions and
  reenactments of Longinus. For many participants, taking part is a personal
  panata — a vow expressed through penance or thanksgiving. [National Museum](https://www.nationalmuseum.gov.ph/exhibitions/nm-marinduque-romblon-area-museum/moryonan/);
  [Philippine Information Agency](https://pia.gov.ph/news/marinduque-prepares-for-moryonan-lenten-rites/)
- Etiquette (good to include in a visitor note): many morions are fulfilling a
  personal vow (panata); ask before close-up photos and never lift a mask. [marinduqueguide.com]
- Kalutang: percussion instrument of two wooden sticks, associated with Gasan; cited
  by NCCA (2011) as intangible cultural heritage. [Wikipedia]
- Kangga Festival: Mogpog. Bila-Bila Festival is featured on the provincial site.
- Luzon Datum of 1911 (Station Balanacan): Mt. Mataas, Brgy. Hinanggayon, Mogpog.
  Origin point of the Luzon Datum, reference for Philippine maps/surveys; NHCP historical
  marker installed August 9, 2011. [NHCP; DOT-MIMAROPA; Senate Bill 1154]

## Municipalities (descriptions safe to use)

- Boac (capital, 1st class): seat of the provincial government; center of commerce
  and education. Municipal Hall at Brgy. Tampus; Marinduque Museum at Brgy. Malusak;
  Moriones Arena. Name from "biak" (divided) — the Boac River splits the town.
  Municipal Hall tel: (042) 311-1378.
- Mogpog: primary gateway via Balanacan Port; origin of the Moriones (1807);
  Kangga Festival; Luzon Datum of 1911; Tarug Caves. Municipal Hall tel: (042) 332-1249.
- Gasan: "cultural nerve center"; home of the kalutang and handicrafts; faces the
  Tres Reyes Islands; Marinduque Airport is in Brgy. Masiga (between Gasan and Boac).
  Municipal Hall tel: (042) 342-1074.
- Buenavista: smallest municipality, at the foot of Mt. Malindig; name means
  "good view" (of Tablas Strait). Municipal Hall tel: (042) 704-0062.
- Torrijos: "summer capital"; Poctoy White Beach; locally grown strawberries.
  Municipal Government Center, Rizal St., Brgy. Poblacion. Tourism Office: 0963-689-5699.
- Santa Cruz (1st class): largest by land area, second most populous; gateway to
  Maniwaya and Polo islands; Bathala Cave (Brgy. Ipil). Municipal Hall tel: (042) 5047-001.

## Health facilities

- Dr. Damian Reyes Provincial Hospital (formerly Marinduque Provincial Hospital),
  Brgy. Santol, Boac, near the Provincial Capitol. Level 1 general hospital
  (internal medicine, OB-GYN, pediatrics, surgery, lab, ECG). PhilHealth-accredited.
  Published numbers vary by source — say "call the hospital's published line / visit
  the ER; verify current number on the provincial government's official page".
- Santa Cruz District Hospital, Santa Cruz — tel (042) 754-4306 (directory; verify).
- Torrijos Municipal Hospital, Torrijos — tel (042) 753-0322 (directory; verify).
- Each municipality has a Rural Health Unit (RHU) / Municipal Health Office and
  barangay health stations — standard PH setup; direct people there for free

## Getting there

- Sea: Balanacan Port (Mogpog) ↔ Dalahican Port, Lucena City (Quezon), ~3 hours by RoRo.
  Operators: Starhorse Shipping Lines and Montenegro Shipping Lines; several daily trips;
  schedules change — check with the operator. Other ports: Cawit (Boac), Buyabod (Santa Cruz).
  Boat link General Luna (Quezon) ↔ Santa Cruz via Maniwaya Island.
- Air: Marinduque Airport, Brgy. Masiga, Gasan. Cebu Pacific (CebGo) resumed Manila flights
  in April 2019; check current schedules with the airline.
